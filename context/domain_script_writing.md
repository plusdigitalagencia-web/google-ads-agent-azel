# Domain Context: Ad Script Writing

> **Purpose:** Guidelines for writing effective paid social ad scripts.

## Core Principles

### 1. Sound Like a Customer, Not a Brand
- Write like someone talking to a friend
- Use exact phrases from customer research
- Avoid marketing speak and corporate language

### 2. Be Specific, Not Vague
- BAD: "I feel so much better"
- GOOD: "I lost 12 lbs in 6 weeks"
- Numbers, timeframes, and specifics build credibility

### 3. Hook in < 3 Seconds
- Pattern interrupt immediately
- Stop the scroll before anything else
- The hook is 80% of the ad's success

### 4. Never Sound Like an Ad
- Sound like content that belongs on the platform
- Would this exist organically? If not, rethink it.

## Script Structure

```
HOOK (0-3 sec)
- Pattern interrupt
- Specific, attention-grabbing statement
- Uses customer language

PROBLEM (3-10 sec)
- Agitate the pain using their exact words
- Make them feel seen and understood
- Don't rush past this

SOLUTION (10-20 sec)
- Introduce product naturally
- Don't be salesy
- Connect solution to their specific problem

PROOF (20-25 sec)
- Specific benefit or result
- Numbers when possible
- Social proof if available

CTA (25-30 sec)
- Clear next step
- Low friction
- Urgency without being pushy
```

## Script Types

### 1. Problem-Solution
Start with the pain, introduce product as solution.
- "If you've ever [pain point], you know how frustrating..."

### 2. Testimonial
First-person "I tried everything until..."
- "I was so skeptical at first, but..."

### 3. Myth Buster
"Everyone says X, but here's the truth..."
- Challenges conventional wisdom
- Positions product as the real answer

### 4. Investigation Arc
"I saw this expert say [X], so I tried..."
- Borrows authority from external source
- Great for products with credibility challenges

### 5. Before/After
"6 months ago I was [state], now..."
- Clear transformation
- Emotional contrast

## Hook Formulas That Work

1. **Controversial statement:** "I'm convinced [industry] hates [customer type]"
2. **Specific pain:** "I've literally [extreme consequence] just by [normal activity]"
3. **POV format:** "POV: You finally find [product] that [benefit]"
4. **Call-out:** "If you have [characteristic], you need to hear this"
5. **Myth bust:** "Everyone says [common belief], but actually..."

## What to Avoid

- Corporate marketing speak
- Vague claims without specifics
- Features without benefits
- Rushing to the product
- Sounding like you're selling
- Generic hooks that could apply to any product

## Quality Checklist

Before finalizing a script:
- [ ] Hook stops the scroll in < 3 seconds
- [ ] Uses exact customer language from research
- [ ] Specific, not vague
- [ ] Sounds like a person, not a brand
- [ ] Clear CTA
- [ ] Would work as organic content
